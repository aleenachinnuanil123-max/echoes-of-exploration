Exploration

They travelled millions of kilometres, explored new worlds, and changed what we know about our solar system. Their missions ended — but their stories did not.

Not forgotten. Still part of the story.

NASA’s missions leave more than discoveries behind. Some machines remain on the Moon, some rest on Mars, and others continue silently through space. This collection introduces the engineering, purpose and scientific legacy of these explorers.

How to explore: Choose the Moon or Mars filter to browse mission artifacts. Open the NASA source links on each card to explore the original mission information.

Moon · Apollo 11 · 1969

Apollo Lunar Module — Eagle's descent stage

The descent stage of the Apollo 11 Lunar Module remained on the Moon after the ascent stage carried the astronauts back to orbit. It helped make the first crewed lunar landing possible.

Moon · Apollo 15 · 1971

Lunar Roving Vehicle

The battery-powered rover extended astronauts’ range across the lunar surface. Three LRVs were left on the Moon, where they remain as evidence of human exploration.

Moon · Apollo science

ALSEP scientific packages

Apollo astronauts deployed scientific instruments that continued collecting measurements after the crews departed. The experiments helped scientists study the Moon’s interior and environment.

Moon · Robotic exploration

Surveyor 3

Surveyor landers tested technologies needed for safe lunar landings. Surveyor 3 landed in 1967; Apollo 12 astronauts later visited it and brought selected parts back to Earth.

Mars · Pathfinder · 1997

Sojourner rover

Sojourner became the first successful wheeled rover to operate on Mars. It examined nearby rocks and demonstrated technologies for mobile planetary exploration.

NASA Mars Pathfinder mission page ↗
Mars · MER · 2004–2010

Spirit rover

Spirit explored the Martian surface and found evidence that water had altered rocks. It became stuck in soft soil and its mission ended after communication was lost.

NASA Spirit mission page ↗
Mars · MER · 2004–2018

Opportunity rover

Designed for a 90-sol mission, Opportunity operated for almost 15 years. Its observations revealed strong evidence that liquid water once shaped parts of Mars.

Mars · Viking · 1976

Viking 1 lander

Viking 1 was the first U.S. spacecraft to successfully operate on the Martian surface. Its experiments studied the soil and searched for signs of biological activity.

Mars · InSight · 2018–2022

InSight lander

InSight studied the planet’s deep interior, measuring marsquakes and investigating the structure of Mars. The mission ended when dust reduced its solar power.

Mars · Mars 2020 · 2021–2024

Ingenuity helicopter

Ingenuity made the first powered, controlled flights on another planet. After rotor damage ended its flight operations, it remained on the Martian surface as a historic aircraft.

A timeline of exploration

1967
Surveyor 3 lands on the Moon and tests landing technologies.
1969
Apollo 11 lands people on the Moon; its lunar module descent stage remains there.
1971
Apollo 15 astronauts use the Lunar Roving Vehicle to travel farther across the Moon.
1976
Viking 1 begins operations on the Martian surface.
1997
Sojourner becomes the first rover to operate on Mars.
2004
Spirit and Opportunity begin exploring Mars.
2018
NASA’s InSight lander arrives to study the Martian interior.
2021
Ingenuity makes its first powered flight on Mars.

Why remember these machines?

An inactive spacecraft is not an unsuccessful spacecraft. Each artifact represents a mission objective, an engineering challenge, and scientific knowledge gained. Studying their stories helps us understand how exploration works — and how one mission prepares the way for the next.
